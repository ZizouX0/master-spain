# English-taught masters in Spain starting January–March 2027: visa-letter cost, how easy it is to get in, and when to apply

Researched on **28 September 2026** by three rounds of parallel research agents (21 agents; about 250 universities and schools checked), using official school and university websites (aggregators only as a fallback).

**What "cost to get the letter" means here:** everything a non-EU student must pay *before* the school issues the official admission / enrolment letter used for the Spanish student visa. That is the application fee plus any deposit, reservation fee or tuition down-payment. In almost every case the deposit is later **deducted from tuition**; it is not an extra cost on top.

**Rules for inclusion:** taught 100% in English, **on campus** (online and blended programmes don't qualify for a student visa), and starting in January, February or March 2027.

**"How easy to get in" scale** (for a typical applicant: bachelor's in a related field, average grades, IELTS ~6.5):

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **5 – Very easy** | Any bachelor's; school's own English test or interview accepted instead of IELTS; no entrance exam; rolling admission; decision in days |
| **4 – Easy** | Any bachelor's; IELTS 6.0–6.5 (or equivalent); CV + motivation letter + interview; no GMAT/GRE |
| **3 – Moderate** | Related bachelor's required, or strict IELTS 6.5–7.0, or portfolio, or limited places, or own admission test |
| **2 – Hard** | Specific bachelor's ranked by grades, very few places, or GMAT/GRE/admission test **and** high English, or work experience required |
| **1 – Very hard** | Top-ranked, low acceptance rate, high GMAT + several years' experience |

No school publishes an acceptance rate for these intakes, so the scores are based on the published entry requirements.

> ⚠️ Fees change and several schools contradict themselves between pages. **Get the amount, and the refund-if-visa-refused rule, in writing from admissions before you pay anything.**

## 8. Things to keep in mind

1. **Official vs private degree.** Only UPC, UPF-BSM, IE, La Salle's MSc Digital Arts and (per EADA) EADA's masters are Spanish *official* degrees. A private or "título propio" master still qualifies for a student visa, but (per ESIC's site) it does **not** count toward Spain's post-study job-search residence permit.
2. **Evening programmes** (IED, GBSB MA courses, ESEI, IAU): confirm with the consulate that the schedule counts as full-time study.
3. **Official degrees need paperwork:** UPC, UPF-BSM, La Salle, IE and Cantabria want your degree **apostilled/legalised** and translated, which takes time.
4. **Before paying**, email admissions and ask: (a) the exact amount needed before the visa letter is issued, (b) whether it is refunded if the visa is refused, (c) the exact start date and last application date, and (d) the English level they require.
