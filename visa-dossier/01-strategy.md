# Strategy: Spanish course first, then a master's in September 2027

## 1. Which visa category a Spanish course falls into

Estudio Sampere is a private language school accredited by Instituto Cervantes. Under the 2025 regulation (Real Decreto 1155/2024), a course there counts as a **"training activity"** (*actividad formativa*, article 52.1.e.2º). A master's counts as **"higher education"** (*estudios superiores*, article 52.1.a). The two categories have different rules:

| Rule | Language school (Estudio Sampere) | Master's / higher education |
|---|---|---|
| How long the permit lasts | The length of the course, **1 year maximum** (art. 55.1) | The whole official length of the programme |
| Extensions (*prórroga*) | **Only 1**, and you need a DELE or SIELE diploma, or proof you've registered for the DELE (art. 55.5) | Up to 2 |
| Work while studying | **Not automatic.** You'd need a separate work permit application (art. 57.1) | Automatic, up to 30 hours a week (art. 57.1–2) |
| Can the permit turn into a master's from inside Spain? | **Yes, with a new application, not an extension.** Art. 54.1 ¶2 lets anyone legally in Spain file a higher-education (52.1.a) application at the oficina de extranjería, or the university files it (art. 54.6). File at least 2 months before your permit expires (art. 54.3) and 2 months before the master's starts. | Not applicable |

**What this means for you (corrected in version 2):** once you're admitted to the master's, you can apply for the new permit **from inside Spain** at least 2 months before your language-course permit ends, or go back to Tunisia and apply at the embassy. An earlier version of this file wrongly said the in-Spain switch wasn't possible; the mock review caught it and the regulation text confirms it.

Also plan your budget with **zero income from work** during the language course.

## 2. Alternatives worth checking

1. **A Spanish course run by a university.** Examples are the University of Salamanca's Cursos Internacionales, the University of Alicante's Centro Superior de Idiomas, and the Centro Complutense para la Enseñanza del Español in Madrid. Guidance published by Spain's migration authority in 2025 (Instrucción SEM 3/2025) treats in-person language courses run by recognised higher-education institutions as higher education. That category allows work, allows 2 extensions, and fits better with a master's. Ask the university in writing whether its visa letter is accepted as *estudios superiores*.
2. **Get admitted to the master's first, then take the university's preparatory course.** Article 52.1.a treats a university's preparatory course (*curso preparatorio / curso cero*) for students it has already admitted as higher education. The visa then covers the prep course and the master's together. This is legally the strongest route. The catch is timing: admissions for September 2027 usually open in early 2027.
3. **Stay with Estudio Sampere.** This works if you accept the constraints in section 1. It's a well-known school, it's Cervantes-accredited, and it prepares students for the DELE exam.

## 3. How a consulate reads a language-course application

Language-course visas get more scrutiny than degree visas, because the officer has to believe you're really coming to study. What persuades them:

- **The course is full-time, intensive and in person**, with the hours per week and exact dates written in the school's letter. At least 20 lessons a week is the norm for intensive courses. Ask the school to confirm this.
- **The whole course is already paid.** The receipt proves the school received the money, not just an invoice.
- **The plan hangs together.** Your current Spanish level, the level you're aiming for (most Spanish-taught master's ask for B2), the DELE exam you'll register for, the master's programmes you're targeting and their language requirement, and a start in September 2027 should all connect.
- **The money is clear:** at least €700 a month blocked for the whole stay (the legal minimum is €600). It should come from a stable account with a history and a source you can explain, such as a parent's salary.
- **You have somewhere to live.** If the whole stay is prepaid, the money you need to show is reduced.
- **You have reasons to come back**, such as family in Tunisia and a professional plan there after the master's. Only say this if it's true.

## 4. Example timeline (change it to match your real course dates)

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Oct–Nov 2026** | Choose the campus (Madrid, Salamanca or Alicante) and the dates. Pay the course and get the admission letter. Start collecting the documents in [02-checklist.md](02-checklist.md). |
| **At least 2 months before the course starts** | Submit your file at BLS Tunis. For a course starting on 1 February 2027, that means **by 1 December 2026 at the latest**. Earlier is better. |
| **Feb–Jun 2027** | Take the course. Register for a DELE B2 session (dates at <https://examenes.cervantes.es>). Apply to master's programmes; many Spanish universities take applications between February and June. |
| **By the end of the course + 15 days** | Your permit ends 15 days after the course does (art. 55.2), unless it has been extended. Leave Spain on time. Staying past it damages your master's application. |
| **Jun–early Jul 2027** | Get your master's admission and pay the fees. Back in Tunisia, apply for the master's visa **at least 2 months before September**. |
| **Sept 2027** | The master's starts. |

## 5. Money

The legal minimum is €600 a month (IPREM 2026). In practice in Tunis, **€700 a month is blocked irrevocably** (agency checklist). Plan on **€700**.

| Period | Amount to block |
|---|---|
| Language course, e.g. 5 months (Feb–Jun) | 5 × €700 = **€3,500**, plus any course fees not yet paid |
| Master's, 12 months (next application) | 12 × €700 = **€8,400**, plus any master's fees not yet paid |

Showing that your family could also fund the master's year makes the whole plan believable.
