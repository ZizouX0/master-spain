# Spain study visa: proofs of the master's for a business graduate

For a Tunisian business graduate following this plan:

- an intensive Spanish course in Spain from February 2027;
- the visa appointment in Tunis in early December 2026;
- then an official master's (Máster Universitario) in business, taught in Spanish and in person, from September/October 2027.

Researched 30 September 2026. Every fact was checked on the university's official page, its official rules, or the RUCT (Spain's official register of university degrees), unless it is marked *to confirm*.

## The idea in one line

Language-course-only visas are often refused with "motif 10": the consulate doesn't believe the purpose of the stay. Your file must show that the Spanish course is **step 1**, and that **a business master's taught in Spanish is step 2**, with written documents from universities.

You don't need B2 Spanish now. What you need is **a university document by 1 December 2026** saying you are admitted (or conditionally admitted), pre-admitted, or pre-registered.

## 2. The best combination

- **Main target: ESIC, MU Dirección de Marketing.** You get a written admission by email, and the €2,000 is refunded if the visa is refused.
- **Backup: UNIE**, conditional admission (Marketing Digital or Analítica de Negocio). It is in Madrid, like ESIC.
- **Public-university document:**
    - the UPV Fase 0 receipt (MUGEPS + one Spanish-B2 master), from mid-November; and/or
    - the Zaragoza "autorización de acceso" (file it in October).

Keep **one main master's and one backup**, all in business and all taught in Spanish. In the motivation letter, name ESIC as the main target.

## 3. The Spanish course

- **Estudio Sampere Madrid, long-term course**: 20 lessons a week, 32 weeks, **Monday 8 February – Friday 17 September 2027**.
    - 2026 price €4,090 + €49 enrolment (*confirm the 2027 price*).
    - It starts any Monday.
    - The visa letter is issued after full payment.
    - If the visa is refused, everything is refunded except €350.
- It is in the same city as ESIC and UNIE, which makes one coherent story.
- **Filing deadline:** at least 2 months before the course starts. For 8 February, the last day to file is **Monday 7 December 2026**.

## 4. Supporting documents (to have by 1 December)

- **Sampere letter**: dates, hours per week, in person, full-time, Cervantes-accredited, fully paid, target level B2.
- **Your current Spanish level**: SIELE in Tunis by 20 October (€145, results within 3 weeks) and/or an Instituto Cervantes Tunis course certificate.
- **DELE B2 registration** in Madrid for 22 May 2027. Registration opens 18 November 2026, costs €204 (2026 price) and is paid online; keep the emailed receipt.
- **Letter from your Tunisian university**, stating:
    - your degree gives access to master's studies in Tunisia;
    - your credits (ECTS if possible);
    - the language of instruction;
    - the exact degree title.

    Also bring an official transcript. Apostille both, then have them translated into Spanish.

- **Bank file** (Central Bank of Tunisia circular 2025-10, art. 6): money for a language course before your main studies can be transferred only if the bank's file covers **both the course and the master's**. Open it with the ESIC/UNIE document.
- **Two-year budget and sponsor proof**:
    - year 1 = course + €700 × months of stay (blocked) + return ticket + insurance;
    - year 2 = the master's tuition (ESIC is expensive) + living costs;
    - include the sponsor's salary or tax papers and 6–12 months of bank statements.
- **One-page timeline** linking each date to a document.
- **Existing visa items**:
    - apostilled criminal record (B3);
    - medical certificate;
    - insurance;
    - accommodation;
    - sworn Spanish translations of every document not in Spanish.

**This week**

- Ask your university for the access letter (Email 5).
- Start the apostilles and sworn translations of your diploma and transcript.
- Book SIELE, or the Cervantes placement test, in Tunis.

**October**

- ESIC admission tests (letter, business case, interview), then the written admission.
- UNIE interview, then the written conditional admission.
- File the Zaragoza "autorización de acceso" (optional).
- Collect the sponsor documents.

**From about 10 November:** check the UPV Fase 0 page every day, then apply and pay on the opening day.

**Wednesday 18 November:** DELE B2 registration (Madrid).

**20–25 November: go / no-go decision**

- **GO** if you hold the ESIC admission or the UNIE letter (ideally plus a public-university receipt):
    1. pay Sampere to get the visa letter;
    2. block the funds;
    3. book insurance and accommodation;
    4. file on 1–4 December (last legal day: Monday 7 December).
- **NO-GO** if you hold no university document:
    - don't file a weak file, because a refusal stays on record;
    - move the course start later (Sampere starts any Monday) and file 2 months before the new date.

## 6. How to avoid "motif 10"

- **Same name everywhere**, exactly as in your passport.
- **Same dates everywhere**: the course dates must match on the Sampere letter, insurance, accommodation, blocked funds and cover letter.
- **Same story everywhere**: one main master's (ESIC) and one backup, both in business and taught in Spanish.
- **Leave out** unanswered emails, unrelated applications, and anything that calls a pre-registration an "admission".
- **Money the officer can trust**: no sudden unexplained deposits, and the sponsor's income must cover both years.
- **A personal motivation letter** covering:
    - why marketing/management follows from your business degree;
    - why Spain, and why a master's taught in Spanish;
    - your career plan;
    - what you'll do if you don't reach B2 (return and apply from Tunisia).
- **At the interview**: know every date and amount, and answer a few questions in Spanish.
